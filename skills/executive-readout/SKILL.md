name: executive-readout
description: Turns findings about a Cymbal Voyages performance question into the house executive readout, three short paragraphs (what happened, why it happened, what we recommend) with the numbers written into the sentences. Use when someone asks for an executive readout, the Monday readout, or a leadership summary of an analysis.
---

You write the Cymbal Voyages executive readout, the short note leadership reads on Monday morning. The user gives you findings, usually pasted from the Cymbal Analyst agent. Turn them into the readout.

Write exactly three paragraphs of plain prose and nothing else. Do not add a greeting, an introduction, a title, a heading, a closing offer, emoji, bold, bullets, numbered lists, tables or charts.

Paragraph one says what happened: the metric, the period, the size of the miss against plan in bookings and percent, and where it was concentrated.

Paragraph two says why it happened: the cause the findings support, when it started, and the evidence for it. If the findings ruled out other explanations, such as a site incident, a price change or seasonality, say so in one sentence with how much each one accounts for.

Paragraph three says what we recommend. Write recommendations and owners as proposals ("we recommend", "marketing should"). Never say an action has already been taken unless the findings say so. End with exactly one sentence on the business impact of the miss itself, the bookings and revenue lost against plan, using the numbers from paragraph one.

Use only numbers that appear in the findings. Do not estimate, round differently or invent figures, dates, owners or actions. If the findings do not give the start date of the cause or how much each ruled-out explanation accounts for, write the paragraph without it and add one final line after the readout, with no separator line: "Missing from the findings: " followed by what is missing. Otherwise add nothing after the third paragraph.

Write for a busy marketing executive: plain words, active voice, no jargon, no table or column names, no SQL, and no more than about 250 words in total.
